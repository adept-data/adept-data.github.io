CISON @ 50th

ABUJA 2026: FIVE DAYS, FIFTY YEARS, TWO NEW PROFESSORS, AND A STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT EXPERIENCE!

When statisticians converge to celebrate fifty years of professional excellence, even the numbers must stand at attention!

Between 12th and 16th October 2026, I had the rare privilege of participating in the 3rd International Conference and 50th Anniversary Celebration of the Chartered Institute of Statisticians of Nigeria (CISON), held in Abuja, the Federal Capital Territory. It was five days of intellectual engagement, professional networking, memorable reunions and remarkable achievements, all woven into one historic celebration.

The golden jubilee, held under the thought-provoking theme, “From Legacy to Leadership: 50 Years of Promoting Statistical Development in Nigeria,” provided an opportunity to reflect on the remarkable journey of the Institute, acknowledge the contributions of its founding fathers and distinguished members, and critically examine the future of statistical practice in Nigeria.

Fifty years of institutional existence is certainly no mean achievement, especially in a country where figures sometimes appear to have political affiliations, and where different institutions can produce conflicting estimates of the same phenomenon without necessarily agreeing on the underlying population parameters!

The celebration was therefore much more than an anniversary. It was a convergence of experience and emerging talent, a meeting point between conventional statistical wisdom and modern analytical innovations, and an opportunity to remind ourselves that the relevance of our profession must be measured not only by its historical achievements but also by its contributions to contemporary national development.

The intellectual journey began with the pre-conference workshop held at 3J’s Hotel, Abuja, from 12th to 13th October 2026. The workshop, themed “Advanced Techniques for Spatial Data Analysis and Interactive Visualization,” was expertly facilitated by the distinguished Prof. C. Edson Utazi.

The workshop attracted an impressive number of participants who demonstrated considerable enthusiasm for the acquisition of new knowledge. Professor Utazi approached the subject with remarkable depth, diligence and professional sincerity, taking participants through important concepts and applications of spatial statistics and interactive data visualisation.

His presentation brought renewed appreciation of the fact that data do not exist in isolation. Beyond their numerical values, observations may exhibit geographical relationships, spatial dependence, clustering and other patterns that conventional analytical approaches might overlook. Understanding these relationships is increasingly essential in public health, environmental studies, agriculture, urban planning, population research and evidence-based policy formulation.

The experience reinforced an important lesson: statistical analysis should not stop at establishing what happened or estimating the magnitude of an occurrence; it should also investigate where it happened, the factors associated with its spatial distribution and the implications of such patterns.

Indeed, after Professor Utazi’s session, even someone who lost his way around Abuja could confidently argue that he was merely conducting exploratory spatial data analysis!

The main conference commenced on Wednesday, 14th October, and continued through Friday, 16th October 2026, at the famous Chida Hotel Event Centre, Utako, Abuja.

The atmosphere was impressive, and the turnout was remarkable. The hall was filled almost to capacity with an assemblage of accomplished statisticians, academics, researchers, government officials, public sector professionals, private sector practitioners and international participants. There were familiar faces, respected mentors, emerging scholars and distinguished personalities whose professional contributions have significantly influenced statistical development in Nigeria.

Looking around the conference hall, I could not help but imagine the magnitude of intellectual capital concentrated within that single geographical location. If statistical expertise were a measurable economic resource, Abuja would certainly have recorded an extraordinary positive shock to its human capital index during those three days!

The conference created an important platform for rigorous intellectual exchanges, technical presentations, institutional reflections and professional engagements. Various sessions addressed issues relating to statistical education, research development, professional practice, institutional capacity and the increasing importance of data-driven decision-making in an evolving world.

Of particular significance was the keynote message delivered by Dr. Godday Uwawunkonye Ebuh, President of CISON, whose reflections on the golden jubilee theme underscored the responsibility of the statistical profession to move beyond its historical achievements towards greater institutional relevance and national leadership.

The address highlighted the need for stronger statistical institutions, adequate professional recognition, sustainable funding, improved statistical education and the development of reliable national data systems. The importance of a credible population and housing census, the strengthening of statistical literacy and the need to integrate statistics more effectively into national development processes were equally brought to the forefront.

The underlying message was compelling: a country cannot consistently make sound policy decisions when the quality, timeliness and reliability of its underlying data remain questionable.

As statisticians, we understand that a poorly defined sampling frame, measurement errors, systematic bias and unreliable estimates can undermine even the most sophisticated analytical model. The same principle applies to national governance. Where the data-generating process is weak, the resulting policy recommendations may be fundamentally misleading, regardless of how impressive the accompanying reports appear.

The golden jubilee message therefore challenged us to consider whether the statistical profession in Nigeria has sufficiently translated its intellectual capacity into practical institutional influence. Fifty years of legacy is commendable, but the next fifty years must be characterised by stronger leadership, professional integrity, innovation and measurable national impact.

Another outstanding moment was the address delivered by the Statistician-General of the Federation and Chief Executive Officer of the National Bureau of Statistics, Prince Adeyemi Adeniran.

His presentation was exceptionally robust, articulate and intellectually engaging. He demonstrated remarkable professional competence, depth of understanding and a commanding appreciation of the strategic role of official statistics in national planning, economic management and governance.

For me, his address was one of the defining moments of the conference. The clarity of his delivery, the confidence with which he articulated his perspectives and the professional authority he brought to the occasion were truly commendable.

If there were a hypothesis test for excellence in professional presentation, I would confidently reject the null hypothesis that his delivery was merely average. The evidence was overwhelmingly persuasive, even without subjecting the audience’s applause to a formal significance test!

His contribution reinforced the importance of credible official statistics and the responsibility of statistical institutions to provide timely, objective and dependable evidence for policy formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

Perhaps one of the most delightful and unexpected developments during the conference was the wonderful news of the promotion of two distinguished academics to the prestigious rank of Full Professor.

Prof. Olatunji Taofik Arowolo of Lagos State University of Science and Technology (LASUSTECH) and Prof. OlaOluwa Simon Yaya of the University of Ibadan both received news of their academic elevations while the conference was ongoing.

What a beautiful coincidence!

Imagine travelling to Abuja for an international conference, with the expectation of exchanging ideas, presenting research, learning new methods and reconnecting with professional colleagues, only for the joyful news of your elevation to the highest conventional academic rank to arrive during the gathering.

The atmosphere of celebration was genuinely heartwarming. Congratulations came from different directions, and the excitement among colleagues was unmistakable. It was a moment that demonstrated the beauty of academic perseverance and the importance of celebrating the accomplishments of others.

In statistical language, one could describe the development as two highly desirable observations occurring within the same short time interval, although I am certain nobody was interested in testing whether the coincidence was statistically significant. The joy was more than sufficient!

For Prof. Arowolo, however, the conference produced an additional remarkable achievement. Shortly after receiving the news of his elevation to full professorship, he was also elected into the Governing Council of CISON, emerging as the first listed elected Council Member after the President and Vice-President.

What an extraordinary sequence of events!

A new professorial rank and a fresh professional leadership responsibility, all within the same conference period. Even the most carefully specified predictive model might have struggled to anticipate such a pleasant combination of outcomes.

I sincerely congratulate Professors Arowolo and Yaya on these well-deserved academic milestones. Their achievements remind younger scholars that the pathway to academic distinction requires intellectual discipline, sustained research productivity, perseverance and an enduring commitment to knowledge.

The conference also witnessed the election of officers into the Governing Council of the Institute, marking another important transition in its leadership structure.

According to the published election results, Dr. Umar Baba emerged as President, while Dr. Ajibade Fatai Bright was elected Vice-President. Prof. Olatunji Taofik Arowolo, Dr. Abuh Musa, Prof. Chinelo Igwenagu and Dr. Kufre Bassey emerged as elected Council Members.

An interesting electoral development involved Dr. Adeoye Akeem and Mrs. Oni Olubunmi, whose contest reportedly ended in an exact tie, with each securing 102 votes, thereby necessitating a subsequent bye-election.

Now, that is one electoral outcome that should naturally interest statisticians!

When two candidates produce identical observed frequencies, one is tempted to investigate the underlying probability distribution. But election officials were certainly more concerned with resolving the tie than entertaining another round of statistical inference!

The election was personally exciting because some of the successful candidates were professional colleagues and friends whose dedication and capabilities I have come to appreciate over the years. I was particularly delighted by the election of my friend, Dr. Abuh Musa, and the emergence of Prof. Arowolo alongside other distinguished members.

I congratulate the newly elected leadership and sincerely hope that their tenure will strengthen the Institute, promote inclusiveness, deepen professional recognition and encourage greater collaboration between academic institutions, government establishments and private sector practitioners.

The anniversary celebration also provided an opportunity to honour deserving members through the conferment of the prestigious Fellowship of CISON. This recognition represents an important acknowledgement of professional accomplishment, dedicated service and contributions to the advancement of statistical science.

Among the distinguished recipients was our own Lagos State Chapter Chairman, Mr. Lawal, whose elevation to Fellowship was a source of considerable pride to members of our chapter. I extend my heartfelt congratulations to him and all the newly conferred Fellows.

Beyond the technical sessions, policy discussions, elections and professional recognitions, the conference had another dimension that I consider equally valuable: the opportunity to strengthen existing relationships and establish new ones.

After all, a statistician who attends an international conference, participates in several technical sessions and returns home without establishing meaningful professional connections may need to revisit his networking sampling strategy!

I had the pleasure of meeting wonderful individuals, including Oluwaseyi from the Federal Ministry of Finance and Riche, another PhD student at the University of Abuja. Our interactions were stimulating and provided fresh opportunities for academic discussions, professional networking and potential future collaborations.

It was equally refreshing to reconnect with my longtime friend, Dr. Bidemi, whose presence brought back pleasant memories of our professional journey. Meeting Dr. Abuh Musa again was another delightful experience, made even more memorable by his successful election into the Institute’s Council.

I was particularly happy to reunite with Dr. Adeoye, a former lecturer at Kwara State Polytechnic during my days as President. Encounters of this nature remind us that professional relationships often transcend institutional boundaries and that the seeds of friendship planted many years ago can continue to flourish long after we have moved into different phases of life.

I also had the privilege of interacting with the highly respected Prof. Osanaiye, a distinguished retired Professor of Quality Control, and Prof. Olubusoye of the University of Ibadan. Their scholarly accomplishments, professional experiences and contributions to the development of statistics remain sources of inspiration.

Of course, the Lagos State Chapter of CISON was not left out of the excitement. Our State Chairman, Mr. Lawal, who was among the newly conferred Fellows, our hardworking Secretary, Mrs. Oni Bunmi, our Treasurer, Riche, Nikolo and several other colleagues were actively present.

Our interactions added a wonderful social dimension to the occasion. Between the technical sessions, professional discussions, photographs and occasional lighthearted conversations, we demonstrated that statisticians are not permanently confined to regression equations, probability distributions and hypothesis testing. We also know how to build friendships, celebrate one another and enjoy meaningful companionship!

One of the strongest impressions I took away from the entire celebration was the urgent need to reposition statistics as an essential instrument of national development rather than a specialised technical function relegated to institutional back offices.

Nigeria faces increasingly complex socioeconomic challenges relating to population growth, unemployment, poverty, healthcare, education, insecurity, infrastructure and public resource allocation. Addressing these challenges effectively requires more than political declarations or intuitive assumptions. It requires reliable observations, appropriate statistical methodologies, transparent measurement systems and credible empirical evidence.

No analytical technique, however sophisticated, can completely compensate for fundamentally unreliable data. A beautifully fitted regression model built on biased observations may produce impressive coefficients, but its policy implications could remain dangerously misleading.

Similarly, statistically significant results do not automatically translate into economically meaningful or socially beneficial outcomes. Statistical significance must be complemented by practical significance, contextual understanding and sound professional judgement.

The conference reinforced my conviction that our profession must continue to emphasise data integrity, methodological rigour, ethical standards and effective communication of statistical findings.

With the growing influence of artificial intelligence, machine learning, big data analytics, geospatial technologies and interactive visualisation, the opportunities before statisticians are enormous. However, these developments also impose greater responsibilities on us to maintain the fundamental principles of statistical reasoning while embracing technological innovation.

Our professional relevance should not be measured merely by the number of datasets analysed, research papers published or conferences attended. It must increasingly be assessed by the extent to which our collective expertise contributes to better institutions, sounder policies, improved public services and measurable societal progress.

The golden jubilee theme, “From Legacy to Leadership,” therefore carries a profound message. The legacy of the past fifty years provides an invaluable foundation, but the credibility of that legacy will ultimately depend on how successfully the profession responds to the challenges and opportunities of the future.

As I reflect on the experience, I am reminded that professional conferences are valuable not only because of the information disseminated but also because of the intellectual curiosity they stimulate, the relationships they strengthen and the new perspectives they bring into focus.

For me, Abuja 2026 produced a remarkably positive balance of professional learning, intellectual engagement, academic celebration, friendship and personal fulfilment.

To the leadership of CISON, the organising committee, distinguished speakers, facilitators, newly elected Council Members, conferred Fellows and every participant who contributed to the success of the golden jubilee, I extend my sincere appreciation.

To Professors Olatunji Taofik Arowolo and OlaOluwa Simon Yaya, congratulations once again on your elevations to full professorship. Your joyful announcements added an unforgettable dimension to the celebration. To Prof. Arowolo and the other newly elected Council Members, I wish you a successful and impactful tenure.

I returned from Abuja with renewed motivation, strengthened professional relationships, new friendships, valuable insights and a deeper appreciation of the responsibilities that accompany our calling as statisticians.

The experience reminded me that, although statistical inference is fundamentally concerned with uncertainty, our collective commitment to professional excellence must never be uncertain.

Five days of engagement, fifty years of legacy, two celebrated professorial promotions, fresh professional leadership and countless meaningful interactions.

If I were to construct a confidence interval around my overall assessment of the conference, I am convinced it would lie comfortably within the region of a highly rewarding professional experience. And if there were an outlier, it would probably be the extraordinary level of joy that accompanied those unexpected promotion announcements!

As CISON begins another chapter in its institutional journey, may its future be characterised by greater professional recognition, stronger intellectual contributions, institutional integrity and meaningful influence on national development.

After all, the purpose of statistics goes far beyond describing what has happened. At its best, it provides the evidence, insight and foresight needed to shape what happens next.

Abuja 2026 was not merely a conference I attended; it was a memorable convergence of legacy, leadership, knowledge, friendship and professional fulfilment.

A worthy experience, indeed!

Long live CISON!

Long live the statistical profession!

Long live the Federal Republic of Nigeria! 🇳🇬
