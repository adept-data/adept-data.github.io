CISON @ 50th Anniversary

ABUJA 2026: FIVE DAYS, FIFTY YEARS, TWO NEW PROFESSORS, AND A STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT EXPERIENCE!

When statisticians gather to celebrate fifty years of professional excellence, even the numbers must stand at attention!

From 12th to 16th October 2026, I had the privilege of participating in the 3rd International Conference and 50th Anniversary Celebration of the Chartered Institute of Statisticians of Nigeria (CISON), held in Abuja, the Federal Capital Territory.

The golden jubilee celebration was organised under the inspiring theme:

“From Legacy to Leadership: 50 Years of Promoting Statistical Development in Nigeria.”

What a fitting theme for an institution that has spent five decades advancing statistical knowledge, strengthening professional standards and contributing to Nigeria’s developmental journey!

Fifty years of statistical excellence is no small achievement, particularly in a country where figures sometimes appear to have political affiliations and where two government agencies may produce different numbers for the same reality!

For five memorable days, Abuja became the meeting point of intellectual giants, distinguished professionals, emerging scholars, policymakers and international participants.

It was not merely a celebration of the past but also an opportunity to examine the present and shape the future of our noble profession.

THE PRE-CONFERENCE WORKSHOP: WHEN SPATIAL STATISTICS TOOK CENTRE STAGE

The intellectual journey began with a two-day pre-conference workshop held at 3J’s Hotel, Abuja, from 12th to 13th October 2026.

The workshop, which attracted an impressive number of participants, focused on the theme:

“Advanced Techniques for Spatial Data Analysis and Interactive Visualization.”

The session was expertly facilitated by Professor C. Edson Utazi, who demonstrated remarkable mastery of the subject, exceptional diligence and admirable professional commitment.

His presentation explored the analytical power of spatial statistics, modern techniques for understanding geographical patterns, and the importance of interactive visualisation in communicating complex statistical findings.

The training was intellectually engaging and practically relevant, reminding participants that modern statistical practice increasingly requires us to understand not only what is happening but also where, why and how different phenomena are geographically connected.

Indeed, after Professor Utazi’s session, even someone who lost his way around Abuja could confidently claim he was conducting exploratory spatial analysis!

The workshop provided an excellent foundation for the main conference.

THE MAIN CONFERENCE: WHEN NIGERIA’S STATISTICAL GIANTS ASSEMBLED

On Wednesday, 14th October 2026, the main conference commenced at the renowned Chida Hotel Event Centre, Utako, Abuja, and continued through Friday, 16th October.

The atmosphere was electrifying!

The conference hall was filled almost to capacity with distinguished statisticians drawn from academia, government ministries, public institutions, private organisations, research establishments and international delegations.

Looking around the hall, one could confidently conclude that a considerable proportion of Nigeria’s statistical intellectual capital had temporarily relocated to Abuja.

If intellectual capacity could generate electricity, the gathering might have contributed meaningfully to the national grid!

The conference featured keynote presentations, technical sessions, strategic meetings, professional discussions and intellectual exchanges addressing several important issues confronting the statistical profession.

THE KEYNOTE ADDRESS: FROM LEGACY TO LEADERSHIP

One of the defining moments was the keynote message delivered by Dr. Godday Uwawunkonye Ebuh, President of CISON, reflecting on the Institute’s golden jubilee theme.

His address challenged the statistical profession to move beyond celebrating past achievements and embrace greater leadership responsibilities in national development.

Among the important issues emphasised were strengthening Nigeria’s statistical institutions, improving professional independence, ensuring sustainable funding, enhancing statistical education and developing a credible national data infrastructure.

The need for a reliable national population and housing census was equally highlighted, alongside the importance of restoring statistics to its rightful place in the educational system.

The underlying message was unmistakable:

Nigeria cannot build a credible future on unreliable figures, outdated information and assumptions dressed in official attire.

Fifty years of legacy must now translate into stronger leadership, institutional reforms and measurable contributions to national development.

The address was a powerful reminder that statistics must move beyond the pages of reports into the corridors where consequential decisions are made.

THE STATISTICIAN-GENERAL’S ADDRESS: A MASTERCLASS IN PROFESSIONAL LEADERSHIP

Another remarkable highlight was the address delivered by the Statistician-General of the Federation and Chief Executive Officer of the National Bureau of Statistics, Prince Adeyemi Adeniran.

His presentation was robust, articulate, authoritative and exceptionally engaging.

For me, it was one of the outstanding moments of the conference.

He demonstrated a profound understanding of the profession and its strategic significance to national planning, policy formulation and development.

His delivery reflected professional confidence, intellectual depth and a strong appreciation of the responsibility statisticians carry in a modern society.

In fact, if there were a statistical test for outstanding professional delivery, I suspect his presentation would have produced a highly significant result, with a p-value comfortably below 0.001!

His message reinforced the urgent need to accord credible statistical evidence its rightful place in governance, economic management and public accountability.

DOUBLE PROMOTION, DOUBLE CELEBRATION: WHEN GOOD NEWS LOCATED TWO SCHOLARS IN ABUJA!

Now, this was undoubtedly one of the most beautiful and emotionally satisfying moments of the entire gathering.

While the conference was ongoing, Professor Olatunji Taofik Arowolo of Lagos State University of Science and Technology (LASUSTECH) and Professor OlaOluwa Simon Yaya of the University of Ibadan received the wonderful news of their promotions to the prestigious rank of Full Professor.

Imagine attending an international conference to acquire knowledge, exchange ideas and strengthen professional relationships, only to receive the life-changing news of your elevation to the pinnacle of academic professorship right there!

What a glorious moment!

The excitement was genuine, the congratulations heartfelt and the joy contagious.

Two distinguished scholars, two remarkable academic milestones and one historic conference!

It was as though Abuja had decided that celebrating fifty years of statistics was not enough; it needed two additional reasons for celebration!

For Professor Arowolo Taofik, however, the story became even more interesting.

Following the joyous news of his promotion, he also emerged as the first listed elected Council Member of CISON, after the President and Vice-President, in the newly constituted Governing Council.

What a remarkable sequence of events!

He arrived at the conference as an accomplished academic, received news of his promotion to full professorship and subsequently emerged with an additional professional leadership responsibility.

One conference, two extraordinary achievements!

Even a sophisticated predictive model might struggle to forecast such a beautiful combination of academic elevation and professional recognition.

I heartily congratulate Professors Arowolo and Yaya on their well-deserved promotions.

May these remarkable achievements inspire greater contributions to research, scholarship, mentorship and the advancement of statistical science.

THE GOVERNING COUNCIL ELECTION: A NEW CHAPTER OF LEADERSHIP

The conference also witnessed the election of officers into the Institute’s Governing Council.

According to the published election results, the following distinguished professionals emerged:

  1. Dr. Umar Baba — President

  2. Dr. Ajibade Fatai Bright — Vice-President

  3. Professor Olatunji Taofik Arowolo — Council Member

  4. Dr. Abuh Musa — Council Member

  5. Professor Chinelo Igwenagu — Council Member

  6. Dr. Kufre Bassey — Council Member

The contest involving Dr. Adeoye Akeem and Mrs. Oni Olubunmi reportedly ended in a tie, with each candidate securing 102 votes, necessitating a subsequent bye-election.

For a gathering of statisticians, an exact electoral tie was certainly an interesting outcome!

Apparently, even the ballot papers decided to demonstrate the practical application of equal frequency distributions!

The election was particularly exciting because some of the successful candidates were people I knew personally and professionally.

I was delighted by the emergence of my friend, Dr. Abuh Musa, alongside Professor Arowolo and other distinguished colleagues.

I congratulate the newly elected leadership and sincerely hope that their tenure will usher in stronger institutional development, professional visibility, inclusiveness and greater opportunities for statisticians across Nigeria.

FELLOWSHIP CONFERMENT: HONOUR TO WHOM HONOUR IS DUE

The anniversary celebration also featured the conferment of the prestigious Fellowship of CISON on deserving members who have distinguished themselves through professional excellence, scholarship and dedicated service.

Among those honoured was our own Lagos State Chapter Chairman, Mr. Lawal, whose recognition brought immense pride and excitement to our chapter.

Such recognition reminds us that diligence, professional commitment and years of meaningful contributions are never wasted.

To all the newly conferred Fellows, congratulations on your well-deserved honours!

BEYOND STATISTICS: THE BEAUTY OF FRIENDSHIP, REUNION AND PROFESSIONAL CONNECTIONS

As enriching as the intellectual sessions were, the social interactions and professional networking opportunities made the experience even more memorable.

After all, what is the value of a statistically significant conference if it produces no meaningful social correlation?

I had the pleasure of making new friends, including Oluwaseyi from the Federal Ministry of Finance and Riche, a fellow PhD student at the University of Abuja.

These interactions provided opportunities for academic engagement, future collaborations and meaningful professional relationships.

It was equally delightful to reunite with my longtime friend, Dr. Bidemi, and Dr. Abuh Musa, whose election into the Governing Council added another beautiful dimension to the occasion.

Meeting Dr. Adeoye, a former lecturer at Kwara State Polytechnic during my days as President, was another special experience.

Such encounters remind us that the relationships we build along our academic and professional journeys often outlive the institutions where they began.

I was also honoured to meet and interact with distinguished scholars, including Professor Osanaiye, a respected retired Professor of Quality Control, and Professor Olubusoye of the University of Ibadan.

These accomplished personalities represent generations of intellectual investment and professional contributions worthy of appreciation.

And, of course, my wonderful colleagues from the Lagos State Chapter of CISON were strongly represented!

Our indefatigable State Chairman, Mr. Lawal, our hardworking Secretary, Mrs. Oni Bunmi, our Treasurer, Riche, the ever-present Nikolo, and numerous other colleagues added warmth, excitement and a strong sense of professional family to the gathering.

Indeed, Lagos did not merely attend the conference; we participated, connected, celebrated and made our presence felt!

MY BIGGEST TAKEAWAY: STATISTICS MUST MOVE FROM THE BACK OFFICE TO THE DECISION-MAKING TABLE

Beyond the technical presentations, professional recognitions, academic promotions and leadership elections, the conference reinforced one fundamental truth:

No serious nation can achieve sustainable development without credible statistics.

The golden jubilee theme, “From Legacy to Leadership: 50 Years of Promoting Statistical Development in Nigeria,” could not have been more appropriate.

It challenged us to acknowledge the contributions of those who laid the foundations of our profession while preparing ourselves to confront the realities of a rapidly changing world.

From traditional survey methods to artificial intelligence, machine learning, big data, geospatial analytics and interactive visualisation, the statistical profession continues to evolve.

We must evolve with it.

We need stronger professional institutions, better integration of academic research with policymaking, increased investment in statistical education and greater recognition of statisticians in national development.

Most importantly, we must insist on the integrity, reliability and responsible application of data.

A nation cannot continue to rely on guesswork and expect precision in its development outcomes.

Statistics is not merely about producing figures; it is about ensuring that figures produce meaningful decisions.

And as statisticians, we must remain committed to that responsibility.

FINAL REFLECTION: FIVE DAYS THAT WERE WORTH EVERY MOMENT

I returned from Abuja with renewed professional motivation, deeper intellectual appreciation, strengthened friendships, fresh professional connections and unforgettable memories.

The conference was more than a gathering of statisticians. It was a celebration of history, scholarship, professional growth, institutional continuity and future possibilities.

To the leadership of CISON, members of the organising committee, keynote speakers, facilitators, distinguished Fellows, newly elected Council Members and everyone who contributed to the success of this historic celebration, I extend my sincere appreciation.

To Professors Olatunji Taofik Arowolo and OlaOluwa Simon Yaya, congratulations once again on your elevations to full professorship. Your good news brought exceptional joy to the conference.

To the newly elected officers, may your leadership advance the noble objectives of CISON and strengthen the relevance of statistics in Nigeria.

And to every emerging statistician, researcher and scholar, may this celebration serve as a reminder that professional excellence is a continuous journey, not a final destination.

Abuja 2026 was not merely a conference I attended; it was an experience I will cherish for years to come.

Five days.

Fifty years of professional legacy.

Two celebrated professorial promotions.

A new Governing Council.

Countless intellectual engagements.

Beautiful reunions, enduring friendships and fresh professional relationships.

One unforgettable experience!

As we celebrate fifty years of CISON, let us remember that the past provides our foundation, the present demands our commitment and the future calls for visionary leadership.

Life may be full of uncertainties, but our commitment to excellence must remain statistically significant!

Long live CISON!

Long live the statistical profession!

Long live the Federal Republic of Nigeria!
